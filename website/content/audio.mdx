## Audio: extract, inspect, preserve

The Audio card separates the recording's audio track from its video.
Extraction produces an artifact for transcription, speaker segmentation or
bitcube analysis. It is not text-to-speech generation.

### Instructions

1. Select a recording and confirm that its media information lists an audio
   track. A silent recording cannot produce a meaningful transcript.
2. In Audio, choose **Extract WAV**. Wait for the operation result before
   selecting the generated artifact.
3. Play the WAV in the app and inspect its spectrogram. Keep the WAV paired
   with the source, not just a screenshot of its frequency plot.
4. Open Transcript for recognition, Voices for speaker work, or bitcube for
   a source-linked audio representation.

### Example workflow

For a meeting excerpt, export the clip in Video first, extract its WAV in
Audio, and use that WAV as the transcript input. Record the clip offset so
timestamps can be related back to the full meeting.

### Tools and outputs

| Contract action | Input | Output |
|---|---|---|
| `wave` | Selected audio track | WAV artifact |
| `spectrogram` | Audio from the selected media | Frequency visualization |

Both tools depend on the installed media toolchain. The full Windows ZIP
bundles FFmpeg and FFprobe with the native components. An absent executable
or absent audio track should remain a visible failure, not an empty success.

### Agents, SDK and Paperboy

Retain codec, sample rate, channels, duration, source offset and file hash
when handing audio to an agent. A spectrogram is a derived visualization;
it is not evidence of recognized words or speaker identity.
SDK consumers should use the artifact returned by the operation, rather
than guess its output path.
